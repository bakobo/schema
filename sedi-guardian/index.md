# SEDI Guardian (`sedi-guardian`)

**Bakobo provisional Tier C profile, version 3.0.0.** This state-issued, registry-bound credential names the guardian as its issuee (`a.i`) and the ward by AID in the separately disclosable `a.ward` block. A holder can withhold that block as its SAID. There is no guardian-to-ward edge. The ward's own [Ward Core](../sedi-ward-core/) credential carries the `NI2I` edge back to this credential, following [Sam Smith's discussion #1550](https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1550) and [Daniel Hardman's ward example](https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/blob/ec307cd74/tests/acdc/test_ward_authz_presentation.py). These diagrams are not Utah policy; the 2.0.0 schema remains resolvable in [`sedi-guardian-2.0.0/`](../sedi-guardian-2.0.0/).

The `scope` list carries the coarse statutory categories formerly named `powers`. The `powers` list now carries the capabilities a guardian may delegate through a separate [Ward Authorization](../sedi-ward-authz/). A verifier must check both the statutory scope and that every delegated capability is within the guardian's powers. The optional `constraints` edge reaches a GCD if richer act constraints are needed. The required `citizen` E1E edge reaches the guardian's own [Core](../sedi-core/) credential; the `authorization` edge reaches the appointing instrument. Status must be checked in the issuer's registry.

[`example.json`](example.json) and the [`examples/`](examples/) gallery illustrate the recognized bases. They are structural examples, not assertions that a particular court or parent has issued these credentials. [`invalid/`](invalid/) covers absent ward, bad scope or powers, and edge errors. [`rules.json`](rules.json) is this profile's governance artifact. `this.i` `@6ge27cya` records the placement and naming decision.
